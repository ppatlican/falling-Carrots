# 0008: Contact between two soft bodies

Date: 2026-10-14. Status: accepted (owner, 2026-10-14: "build it"; below ~0.8 a tall soft stack may lean over and a fast drop may bounce off, judged by "nothing inside, comes to rest") and built the same day; Results and Known limits below. Milestone 3b, the solver task before the later slices (STATUS Next item 9).

## Context
A soft body dropped onto, or set on, another soft body sank into it (ADR 0007 Known limits). Measured on the GPU before this ADR (`body_probe` with every body set soft by `set=carrot.stiffness=S`, `--disable-vsync`, RTX 3080 Ti): at 0.5, `stack5` ended with bodies 0.70 px apart at 27 px/s, a 3-jelly stack (`boxstack` before its drop) had collapsed into itself by frame 60 (409 members inside, members up to 95 px out of shape), and the dropped body ended beside the stack. At the shipped 0.95 up to 156 of the dropped body's members went inside during the landing and it ended turned over.

### What the diagnosis found
A CPU repro (two soft 8 × 5 or 16 × 10 bodies stacked at rest, nothing dropped) went red the same way, so the impact isn't needed: a body resting on a soft body is enough. Found by switching parts of the contact off one at a time and logging the members involved:

1. **A soft member is pushed on its own, and its region can't hold it.** A contact pushes a member by half the pair's overlap per iteration (`CONTACT_RELAX` × the 50/50 density share), while its region pulls it back by k = 1 − (1 − stiffness)^(1/8) per iteration: 0.31 at 0.95, 0.08 at 0.5, 0.03 at 0.2. So the surface members that carry the contact move far out of line with their neighbours. The upper body's bottom row bunched and spread, its members slipped between the lower body's, and it draped over the lower body like a blanket (CPU, 16 × 10 at 0.5: the upper body 52–86 px wide against 30, members flung to the walls at up to 130 px/s, lattices interleaved). A rigid carrot of the same size on the same soft body pressed it just as flat (to 7 px of its 18) with nothing inside and at rest: the carrot spreads the push over all its members (shape matching), the soft body didn't.
2. **The load leaned over on a soft base.** The lower soft body took half of every contact (no `STACK_K` with a soft side, ADR 0005 item 11), so it squashed under the upper one: at 0.95 by ~3 px, at 0.5 to 40% of its height. Two lattices resting on each other slide half a spacing to nest (the pairs' pushes have a sideways part until they do), which leaves the load ~1 px off centre, and on a base that soft the load leans. A soft-foundation estimate (restoring arm w² / 12δ against the load's height) gives 0.06 rad for the 16 × 10 pair at 0.95 (measured 0.063) and says a stack can't stand below ~0.5. This was the jitter: an exponential lean (speed ×e every ~20 frames, the shipped 0.95 included) until the top slid off.
3. **The push-out from inside a body misfires at corners** (ADR 0003 item 11). A corner member's way out is the 45° diagonal, so a point 1.7 px below the corner of the body above, 0.6 px in from its side, read 0.22 px "inside" that body although it is 0.7 px below its bottom face. Between misaligned soft corners it fired on 2–27 members every iteration at rest. A member took that push on its own and its body's shape took it back; once item 1 below shares pushes over a region, it moved the whole patch, and a soft stack 2 px off centre crept sideways for good (CPU 0.02 px/s, GPU 0.1 px/s) until it slipped.

Ruled out by the same switches: the region rotations in the push-out (the body's rotation instead changed nothing), friction (off, it got worse), the edge-row rule and the swept contacts (they never fired), stop-not-launch (off, it got worse).

## Decision
All of it is for a pair of members in two different soft bodies. A soft body against a rigid body, a grain or water, and a soft body's own folds (self-contacts), behave exactly as before; so do rigid bodies.

1. **A soft member shares what another soft body does to it over its region.** In each iteration, after the contacts, each member of a soft body hands what it got from other soft bodies (their contacts, the push-out, and their friction) out in equal parts to the members of its region, itself included, and is moved by the shares it gets from the members of its own region instead of its own: member i moves by Σ over r in its region of push_r / |r's region|. A contact then moves a patch of the surface together, like a small rigid body, instead of one member; the body still dents and bends. Same idea as ADR 0003 item 12 (a soft body's pressure pushes made rigid), but per region.
   - **Shares, not a mean**: with each member taking its region's mean push, members near an edge (smaller regions) counted for more, and an offset soft stack crept sideways. Shared this way the body's total push is kept.
   - **The friction goes with the push**: shared without it, the contact row was held by friction while the patch behind it moved, and three 8 × 5 bodies stacked at 0.2 rocked sideways in turn, growing from 0.05 to 4 px/s within 7 s (CPU).
   - **Not what a rigid body does to it**: shared, the pushes and friction from two carrot pillars let a jelly plank slide off them into the gap (`droop`, and with the push-out gate below, `test_jelly_plank_bends_over_a_gap`).
2. **Two soft bodies share a contact with `STACK_K`, as rigid bodies do** (`body_stack_weight`). The lower one is heavier by the height between their centroids, so it holds the upper one up instead of giving way under it. A soft body against a rigid body or a grain keeps the density share (ADR 0005 item 11), so a carrot still squashes a jelly 9%.
3. **Between two soft bodies the push-out acts only inside the nearest member's lattice cell** (`in_lattice_cell`, within `SOFT_CELL` 0.55 spacing of it along both axes of its turned lattice). On a square lattice a member's cell is the part of the body nearest it, so a point is inside the body only if it is in the cell of its nearest member; this stops the corner misfire (3 above). A little over half a spacing, so a point in the middle of a lattice square, which the push-out exists for, still counts.

GPU: one `vec2` per particle (`body_push`, binding 28 in `common.glslinc`, shader stamp v19): `solve_delta` writes each member's share (`contact_delta` returns what came from other soft bodies, `body_share`, and counts the member's region, `region_n`, in the loop it already runs); `solve_apply` adds the shares of a soft member's region and takes off its own (a neighbour loop as `body_region_goal`'s, for soft members only); `solve_pressure` clears it before a substep's first apply. CPU reference: `ParticleStep._share_pushes_over_regions`, `in_lattice_cell`, `body_stack_weight`, `_contact_delta`.

## Known limits
- **Below ~0.8 a soft stack can lean over, and a fast drop can bounce or roll off** (accepted by the owner, 2026-10-14). A soft base squashes under the load, and the soft-foundation estimate above says stacks of 0.5 and softer can't stand for long. At 0.2 a falling pile can still be moving after 30 s, and two jellies on each other lean, slide apart and then creep (next item).
- **A lopsided soft body creeps sideways on its own** (not from this ADR: the same on main). A 16 × 10 jelly at 0.2 let go tilted on the floor slides at 0.047 px/s for good (CPU, identical before and after); on the GPU two jellies at 0.2 that had slid apart crept at 0.5–0.6 px/s. Probably the region goals: each member takes the mean of its regions' goals, which doesn't keep momentum when the regions differ in size (FastLSM splits each particle's mass over its regions for this). STATUS Known issues.
- **The push-out still misfires at corners against a rigid body** (rule 3 is for two soft bodies only, as the jelly plank on carrot pillars needs it).
- **Cost**: up to ~0.35 ms a frame for a 2000-member jelly, whether or not it touches another soft body. A per-body flag set when a member touches a soft body could skip the loop for the rest; not built.

## Considered and rejected
- **The rigid share (`STACK_K`) alone for soft pairs**: the jellies rest, but towers of soft boxes still flip (GPU, 0.5: `stack3` and `stack5` turned π).
- **Sharing over regions alone, without `STACK_K`**: on the GPU the towers flipped and a 3-jelly stack still collapsed (523 inside).
- **FleX-style contact normals** (the pair's direction reflected about the other body's outline normal when it points inward; Macklin et al. 2014, section 5): worse on the CPU (14–20 inside, the upper body spread to 95 px).
- **Each member taking its region's mean push**: the stack crept sideways (rule 1, shares).
- **A minimum distance between members of one region** (0.6 spacing, so a crushed soft body can't fold through itself): it held a 3-stack at 0.2 on the CPU, but on the GPU it made more members end inside at 0.2 and made jelly pairs at 0.5 lean 0.3 rad.
- **Sharing what rigid bodies do too, and the push-out gate against rigid bodies**: the jelly plank slid off its carrot pillars (rule 1).
