# 0012: Plasticity, a soft body that keeps its dents (Milestone 3b)

Date: 2026-10-16. Status: proposed; built and verified the same day (Results), waiting for the owner to play it and decide the dough row (item 8, Owner's call). **2026-10-17: the dough row is mochi now** (ADR 0017; owner: "dough will be mochi-like"), so no shipped row has plasticity. The code stays for any soft cluster row with `plasticity` above 0 (the panel's plasticity slider, `set=jelly.plasticity=`, `@plastic`); the Owner's call questions about the dough row are superseded. The next 3b slice (STATUS Next item 9): dough, a soft body that keeps the shape it is pressed or dropped into, where jelly springs back.

## Context
What is decided already (ADR 0002 item 9, SPEC 2.2): `plasticity` is a per-material column, 0 to 1, for cluster materials, in the last free float of the material row (`Material.more.w`, so the row stays 80 bytes), and it edits `rest_offset` toward the shape the body is in now, seen in the body's frame, with no new per-particle field. Shipped materials keep plasticity 0.

What the rest shape feeds, checked before planning:
- **The push-out** (ADR 0003 items 7-11) reads each member's way out of the rest shape, `rest_offset.zw`, worked out once by `ParticleStep.rest_outline` when the body is made or split (on the CPU, uploaded by `ParticleSim.make_body` and `plan_split`). A rest shape that moves leaves it stale.
- **Regions** (ADR 0005) and **self-contacts** are found from rest offsets each iteration: the members whose rest offsets are within the region radius (1.5-2 spacings) match together, and pairs further apart collide. Nothing is stored, so a moved rest shape is read as it is, but a lattice stretched past the radius loses its links and one squeezed tight packs more members into a region.
- **Soft-soft contact** (ADR 0008) shares a member's push over its region, and its push-out acts inside the nearest member's lattice cell (`SOFT_CELL` 0.55 spacing on the turned lattice); grains beside a soft body use the same cell (ADR 0009).
- **The eraser's split** (ADR 0004): on the GPU, `ParticleSim.plan_split` builds the pieces and their outlines from a CPU copy of each body's rest offsets (`_bodies[id].rest`), made when the body was spawned. Once the GPU edits the rest offsets that copy is wrong, and a split would write outlines of the old shape.
- `rest_outline` and `body_pieces` count a member's rest neighbours within 1.5 spacings: fewer than 8 is a boundary member, and pieces are joined through them.

### What a "deformation" to keep is: measured on the CPU
A first plan used each member's error against the mean of its regions' goals, the quantity the regions pass removes. Measured on resting and loaded jellies (CPU reference, stiffness 0.2-0.95, error over a member's per-substep gravity sag g dt^2 / k), that error is a force, not a shape: inside a body it is about one sag in every row, however tall (a 20-row column: rows at 0.2-1.7), and all of a load sits in the rows a contact holds, the floor row (4-15 sags, growing with the weight above) and the row under a carrot (6-11). A smooth squash doesn't show at all: a member's regions above and below cancel. So it can't keep a dent (with a yield of 10 sags, a carrot set on a 12 x 8 jelly at 0.5 left its rest shape 3% lower; dropped 40 px, 3% flatter), and the load a resting body carries sits in its bottom row whatever its rest shape is, so a yield under it crushes that row for good.

The body frame does see a squash: a member's offset from the body's best fit (the rigid motion `body_match` finds), e = R^T (p - c) - (q - c0). With the yield at half a spacing, the same carrot left the rest shape 21% lower and a 40 px drop 23% flatter, and a 30 x 20 dough settled once, in its first 2 s, then kept its rest shape (CPU prototype, stiffness 0.5, plasticity 1; at 0.2, 0.8 and 0.95 too).

## Decision
1. **Column.** `plasticity`, 0 to 1, a required cluster column (`CLUSTER_FIELDS`, like `region`), packed at offset 76 (`Material.more.w`), with a slider for cluster materials shown below stiffness 1 (`PANEL_FIELDS`; `SOFT_ONLY` in `ui/parameter_panel.gd`), where it is read. Shipped jelly, carrot and cork have 0, and a material at 0 runs nothing new.
2. **Flow** (per substep, after the solver iterations, for members of a soft body whose material has plasticity). A member's offset from its body's best fit, in the rest frame: e = R^T (p - c) - (q - c0), with (c, R) the body record's frame and c0 its rest centroid, as `body_match` left them in the last iteration. Past the yield, `PLASTIC_YIELD` 0.5 spacing, its rest offset moves toward where it is by the plasticity's share of the excess per 1/60 s, k = 1 - (1 - plasticity)^(dt 60) (the stiffness formula of ADR 0002 item 7): dq = k (1 - yield / |e|) e, at most `PLASTIC_STEP` 0.1 spacing per 1/60 s. This is Mueller et al. 2005's plasticity (section 4.5: a deformation past c_yield moves the plastic state by c_creep, bounded by c_max, with det S_p = 1 to keep volume) applied to the rest offsets themselves instead of a plastic matrix S_p per cluster, which would need the original shape and a matrix per region.
3. **Caps instead of Mueller's c_max and volume normalisation**, which need the original shape: rest offsets less than `PLASTIC_LINK` 1.5 spacings apart (the rest lattice's links, as `rest_outline` and `body_pieces` count them) stay in the band [`PLASTIC_GAP` 0.7, 1.5] spacings, whatever both members of a pair do in the same substep (`_plastic_room`, `plastic_room`): more than a step inside the band, a move stops a step short of either end; within a step of the near end a member may not move toward that neighbour at all (u.d >= 0), and within a step of the far end it must move toward it by at least |d|^2 (u.d <= -|d|^2). Then |u + d_i - d_j|^2 = |u|^2 + 2 u.(d_i - d_j) + |d_i - d_j|^2 can't shrink, or grow, past the band. Checked only against the neighbour's old rest offset (the first version), two neighbours sliding the same way could each pass and still close on each other by ~step^2 / distance a substep: on the GPU a dough's floor row crept to 0.62 spacing. So the lattice keeps every link (regions of radius 1.5 keep their members, the outline's boundary test and the eraser's pieces stay right), doesn't fold onto itself, and shortens at most to 0.7 of a spacing along a line. Pressed further, a body stays elastic there.
4. **The outline follows** (in the same substep, where a rest offset moved): each member whose hash window (the 3 x 3 cells the solver loops over) holds a member that moved is sorted again on the new rest shape, as `rest_outline` sorts: fewer than 8 rest neighbours within 1.5 spacings is a boundary member, whose outline point is half a spacing out along the sum of the directions from its neighbours; an inner member takes the nearest boundary member's point in its window that lies within `PLASTIC_REACH` 3 spacings of it on the rest shape, and with none keeps its way out, moved with it. The window holds the members near it now, which in a crumpled body can be far apart on the rest shape: without the reach an inner member of a 2000-member dough at stiffness 0.2 took a point 32 px across the body. Unlike `rest_outline`, a boundary member always takes its own point (`rest_outline` may give it a nearer neighbour's at a concave corner); on a box the two give the same outline. Taken from neighbours' points instead (as `rest_outline` walks), a point a boundary member left behind would go on being handed round among inner members as the nearest, inside the moved outline.
5. **The eraser's split reads the rest offsets back** (`ParticleSim`): for a body of a material that had plasticity this session (`_ever_plastic`), the split readback adds `rest_offset`, and `plan_split` builds the pieces and outlines from it (`rest_from_readback`) and keeps it as the body's copy. Other bodies split as before.
6. **Regions, soft-soft contact and grains** need nothing new: they read the rest offsets as they are, and item 3 keeps the lattice's links and spacing in the band they were built for. Checked in Results: dough on dough, dough on sand, dough in water.
7. **GPU:** three per-particle passes after each substep's last solver iteration (`plastic` in the overlay), dispatched only while a body of a soft material with plasticity exists: `body_plastic` (items 2-3; its move into `body_push`, free after the last iteration), `body_outline` (item 4; each boundary member's new outline point into `scratch`, free until `velocity_xsph`) and `body_rest` (writes `rest_offset`), sharing `plastic.glslinc`. Each reads its neighbours' data from the pass before, so no member reads another's half-written rest offset. No `Params` field (it is full) and no new binding; `common.glslinc` changes only in the `Material` comment, and the shader stamp moves to v22. CPU reference: `ParticleStep._plastic_flow`, `_plastic_room`, `_plastic_outline`. Constants: `SimParams.PLASTIC_*`.
8. **A dough material (toolbar row): added, the owner decides whether it stays.** `dough` (id 6): cluster, stiffness 0.8, region 1.5, plasticity 1, density 1.1, friction 0.6, drag 0.6, beige `#e6c88f`. Why add it now: dough is in the v1 roster (SPEC 3) and Milestone 8 cooks it; the owner can play plasticity without editing jelly and resetting it; and the toolbar shows what the slice does. Why stiffness 0.8: a dough keeps only what is past the yield, and at 0.5 and below a body's own elastic sag under gravity (a 16 x 10 at 0.5 rests 8 px under its 18) is past it already, so a soft dough bakes in its own slump at once, loaded or not (Results). Against adding it: a toolbar slot before the reaction that makes dough (flour + water, Milestone 7), and its id is fixed from then on (a data change to move, and there are no saved scenes yet). Removing it is deleting the row.

## Known limits
- **A dough squeezes, it doesn't spread.** With no volume normalisation (item 3), a press compresses the rest lattice down to 0.7 spacing instead of pushing it out sideways, and stops there: a 16 x 10 dough under a 16 x 10 carrot (squashed to 10 of its 18 px) keeps a rest shape 15 px tall, the same as its own weight leaves it at stiffness 0.8. Deep dents need dough to flow sideways. Option, not built: scale the flow's target by the body's area change (Mueller's det S_p = 1 at the body level; `body_match` would add the determinant of A_pq A_qq^-1, one float per body in a new buffer).
- **A soft dough bakes in its own sag.** At stiffness 0.5 and below a body sags past the yield under its own weight (ADR 0002, ADR 0005's slump), so it settles once to its slumped shape, loaded or not. Hence dough at 0.8.
- **A 2000-member soft body dropped at full speed can squeeze its lattice** (intermittent, GPU `softcap`): at 0.5 one run in two had a rest pair at 0.90 px (0.45 spacing), at 0.2 down to 0.16 px, and boundary members' way out up to 2 px off; other runs held 1.49-1.50. Likely members passing more than a hash window in a substep, so a rest pair approaches unseen. Nothing launches or sticks; it comes to rest (0.1-0.2 px/s) and creeps less than 1 px in 2 s afterwards. Smaller bodies held the band in every run.
- **A strip draped over an edge keeps drooping** while gravity bends it past the yield (`fold`, `droop`: up to 0.9 px of rest movement in 2 s at 0.8), until it hangs or lands. Dough-like, but a plank of dough over a gap sags further than a jelly.
- The GPU's outline sort reads only the hash window: a member whose rest neighbours are stretched more than a window away (extreme elastic stretch) can be sorted as a boundary member for a substep.

## Considered and rejected
- **The error against the regions' goals** (the regions pass's own residual, Context): a force, not a shape; it keeps no visible dent and crushes a resting body's floor row.
- **Mueller's per-cluster plastic matrix S_p**, rest shape S_p (x0 - c0): needs the original rest shape and a matrix per region, new per-member fields ADR 0002 ruled out.
- **Recomputing the outline on the CPU** (read back the rest offsets every few frames, `rest_outline`, upload): frames of latency while the shape moves, and ~5-10 ms of GDScript per 2000-member body each time.
- **A band checked only against the neighbour's old rest offset** (item 3's first version): pairs crept through it.
- **Stopping the flow where a member's lattice is stretched past 1.75 spacings** (to keep unseen neighbours out of it): the 2000-member body squeezed further (0.49 px) and the outline went 32 px off; the pass cost three times as much. Replaced by the rest-shape reach on outline points (item 4).
