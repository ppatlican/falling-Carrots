# 0003: Bodies in liquid, and the push-out from inside a body

Date: 2026-10-12. Status: accepted, implemented in the CPU reference and on the GPU (Milestone 3 step 4 and the sand push-out the owner chose, 2026-10-12: "a grain inside a body is pushed toward the nearest edge of the body's rest shape, using the body's position and rotation from shape matching"). Written before the code. Items 2 (level, compression), 4 (reference pressure, wet floor) and 6 (drag) were changed or added by the CPU tests, and item 4 (wet face, surface drop) by the GPU probe, as each says; item 12 (a soft body's pushes made rigid) was added after the owner chose it, 2026-10-12, to fix the first Known limit. The measurements are in "Decided by the tests" below.

## Context
ADR 0002 left two things open:
- **Body–liquid coupling (step 4).** Water passes into and fills bodies: body particles are left out of the density projection grid, of water's PBF density, and of the contact rule for body–liquid pairs.
- **Grains inside a body.** A body's lattice spacing is the contact distance, so a grain pushed into a lattice square sits at about 0.7 spacing from four body particles, whose push-outs cancel. An 8 × 6 box dropped 72 px onto sand ends with 7 grains inside on the CPU reference; dropped 100 px on the GPU, 16 (ADR 0002 Known limits). Liquid has the same problem.

What the grid does today, which constrains step 4:
- The density projection grid (`hydro.glslinc`) pushes only liquid particles, by −∇p dt². Each water particle is held up by the grid's gradient, so water doesn't press on what it touches. A body resting on water only gets back the part of its own weight that it squeezes into the water, whatever its density: contacts alone give no buoyancy.
- Cells that are mostly sand are bed: a wall that holds the water's weight (dp/dn = ρg n), with p continued hydrostatically into the pores. A dry bed carries that pressure too (no liquid feels it there).
- Cells are 16 px. A carrot box of 8 × 6 particles is 16 × 12 px, under one cell.

## Decision
**The grid sees bodies (step 4)**
1. **Two grid channels for body particles.** `hydro_splat` adds each body particle to a body-volume sum and, at min(ρ_b, 1), to a body-weight sum; `hydro_phi` becomes a `vec4` per cell (liquid, powder, body volume, body weight) and `hydro_acc` four integers per cell. `hydro_powder_fill` (the drawing) still reads only powder. `hydro_solve` keeps one `vec2` per cell in shared memory, (level, bed fill), and reads a cell's own compression and room and the cell above's fullness from `hydro_phi` when it sets up the cell's row, so it still needs 12 B per cell (16 KB is the Vulkan minimum per workgroup).
2. **Body volume fills cells; body weight sets the level.** A body particle sits on the rest-spacing lattice, so it fills a cell as much as a water particle at rest. In the solve:
   - the **bed test** counts its volume: a cell is a wall that holds the water's weight when powder × `HYDRO_POWDER_VOL` + body ≥ `HYDRO_SOLID`, so water around a big body has hydrostatic pressure and isn't sucked into it;
   - the **bubble pull** counts its volume, in the cell above (`covered`) and in the cell's own room (liquid + powder × `HYDRO_POWDER_FULL` + body), so a gap next to or under a body is closed and the body itself isn't a bubble to fill;
   - **compression** counts its volume (liquid + body over 1), so a film of water under a body is pushed out. Without it a carrot sunk onto the floor rested tilted 0.25 rad on a one-particle film of water and slid along the floor at ~2 px/s;
   - the **air test and the free surface** use the cell's level, `hydro_level`: liquid + body weight × min(liquid / `HYDRO_AIR`, 1). A floating body displaces its own weight of water, so a cell it floats in reads as level as the water beside it. With the body left out of the level, a cork's cell (liquid 0.36, body 0.44) read lower than its neighbours (0.6); p fell under the cork, and wherever it moved, its new cell's p fell too, so the pressure on its trailing side pushed it on: it drifted at 2 px/s and never stopped. The weight counts only where the cell holds liquid, so a dry body's cells stay air and don't read as a column of water.
3. **Water's PBF density counts body particles at share 1** (they fill a water particle's room), as grains count at `LIQUID_POWDER_SHARE`. They add to the constraint's gradient but don't move with it.

**Water presses on bodies (buoyancy)**
4. **Pressure on a body's wet surface.** Once per substep, before the solver iterations (with the grid's push on liquids, `solve_pressure`), each body particle with liquid neighbours is pushed by the grid pressure at its surface:
   Δx = −(p(x + ½ spacing · ĝ) − p_ref) · g · dt² / (ρ_b · spacing),
   where g is the particle's **wet face**: the sum over its liquid neighbours of the unit vector to each, weighted 1 out to 1.5 spacings and fading to 0 at H, over 1 + √2 (`body_wet_face`). It reads 1 for a flat face of liquid on the rest lattice (one neighbour straight on, two diagonal) and √2 along the diagonal at a corner wet on two sides, wherever the water sits within 1.5 spacings. ĝ is its direction, and ρ_b the body's material density (water's density is 1, as everywhere in the grid until per-material liquid density, Milestone 3b). Pressure under p_ref pushes nothing (the push never pulls a body toward the water). Summed over a body this is ∮ p n dA over its wet surface: a submerged body is pushed up by the weight of the water it displaces (Archimedes), a dry body by nothing, and a floating body only below the waterline, so it rights itself. The sample point is the body's outline, half a spacing out from the particle, so the body's height counts once; at the water particles' positions instead, a column of N particles gets (N + 1)/N of its buoyancy. Below the last row of cell centres the sample is continued hydrostatically (`body_p_sample`).
   - *Changed by the GPU probe:* the first version summed spiky kernel gradients over their value for a flat lattice face (`body_face_grad`). Water by a body sits ~2–2.5 px off it and not on a lattice, and the spiky gradient falls steeply over that range, so it read 0.55–0.64 of a flat face, and an 80 × 40 cork floated at 0.71 of its height instead of ~0.5 (`body_probe waterbig`).
   - **The reference pressure p_ref.** A uniform pressure over a closed surface adds nothing, but deep down it is large: at the floor of a 30k pool (~190 px) the push on each wet particle would be ~2 px per substep, past the half-spacing cap on a correction, so the top and bottom pushes would both be cut and cancel, and a jelly would be squeezed. So a body closed in by liquid, grains, other bodies, walls and the floor is pushed relative to its lowest wet-surface pressure: about ρg × its height. A body with a boundary member in air (nothing but its own body within 1.5 spacings, away from the walls; a boundary member's `e` is half a spacing long) is floating, and its p_ref is 0. Referred to its lowest wet pressure instead, a floating cork that bobbed up until only its bottom row was wet lost all its lift at the top of each bob and kept bobbing at ±8 px/s; smoothing p_ref over 0.1 s made it worse. A dry body's p_ref is +∞ (nothing presses on it).
   - **p_ref is at least g × `BODY_SURFACE_DROP` (2.5 px)**, the height of the grid's free surface (where p reaches 0, as a cell's liquid crosses `HYDRO_SURFACE`) over the surface of the top water particles. With p_ref 0, a floating body's pressure is absolute and it floats level with the grid's surface: an 8 × 6 cork showed 0.16–0.38 of its height under the water's top particles, not 0.5. A max, not a switch at p_min = 0: switched, a 16 × 10 cork whose top went awash (no member in air, p_min ~0) lost the drop, popped up, got it back and sank again, heaving ±4 px for 15 s (`body_probe waterbox`). Deep bodies (p_min ≫ g × 2.5) don't feel it.
   - p_ref is the previous substep's: `solve_pressure` takes the minimum over the body's members (wet: their p; a boundary member in air: 0; others: +∞) with an integer `atomicMin` on the float's bits (p ≥ 0, so they order as integers) into the record's last word, and the first body pass of the next substep moves it into `p_ref`. The record's `vec4 rest` becomes rest centroid (`vec2`), `p_ref` (`float`) and that minimum (`uint`), still 48 B.
   - **The floor under a wet body is wet.** A body particle within 1.5 spacings of the floor (its bottom row rests half a spacing up), of a body that was wet in the last substep (`info.w` flag), counts the floor below it as a face of liquid at the grid's pressure there: water seeps under a body. Without it a cork let go on the floor of a pool, wet only on its top and sides, got no net push and stayed there.
5. **Body–liquid contacts follow the body rule with mass weights.** Pairs closer than one spacing are pushed apart, the share by density as between bodies (the light side moves more), with no friction and no `STACK_K`. The pair runs the ordinary friction with the smaller of the two frictions, which is 0 because water's `friction` is 0; a liquid with friction (Milestone 3b) needs this made a rule. A liquid particle's contact push stays averaged over its contacts. Grains keep `POWDER_LIQUID_SHARE` and `POWDER_LIQUID_REACH`.
6. **Bodies don't get wet, but water drags them.** `WET_SLIP` (friction lost among liquid neighbours) stays a powder rule: a body's friction against grains and other bodies doesn't change under water. A body particle's velocity relaxes toward its liquid neighbours' kernel-weighted velocity at `BODY_LIQUID_DRAG` 20 per second, scaled by how much liquid is around it (`body_liquid_drag`, as `powder_liquid_drag` for grains). Only wet particles feel it, so a body's drag grows with its wet surface while its mass grows with its area. Without it a floating cork heaved at its natural period (~0.8 s, √(g / draft)) and never settled, and a cork rising from the floor capsized at the surface. At 40 per second a rising cork rolled over under water. The water feels no reaction (as for grains).
12. **A soft body's pressure pushes move it as a rigid body.** Between the pushes and their apply, each body whose stiffness is under 1 has its members' pushes (after the half-spacing cap) replaced by their rigid part: the mean push plus a turn about the members' centroid, θ = Σ r × δ / Σ |r|², linearised, each clamped again to half a spacing (`body_project.glsl`, one 128-thread workgroup per body like `body_match`, in its own `bodies` pass between `solve_pressure` and its `solve_apply`; `ParticleStep._project_body_pushes`). The pushes on a wet surface are relative to the body's top (p_ref), so on a 20 px tall jelly the bottom and lower sides are pushed at up to ~9 g and the top hardly at all: their sum is the buoyancy and their torque the righting moment, but the rest squeezed the jelly from the sides, and water got in where it buckled. Members share a material, so their masses are equal. Rigid bodies are left alone, since matching at stiffness 1 takes out the rest within the iteration; skipping them keeps their behaviour exactly as before. The pass reads and writes `scratch`, which `solve_pressure` already fills, so `common.glslinc` doesn't change (stamp stays v17).

**Push-out from inside a body (the owner's option 1)**
7. **Each member stores the way out of its body's rest shape**: `e`, the vector from its rest offset to the nearest point of the rest shape's outline, in the body's rest frame. The outline runs half a spacing outside the boundary members (where a lattice cell ends). It is worked out once, on the CPU, when the body is made (`ParticleStep.rest_outline`): boundary members are those with fewer than 8 members within 1.5 spacings; each one's outward normal is the sum of the directions from its neighbours to it; every member takes the nearest outline point among those its neighbours took (a multi-source walk over the member graph from the boundary inward, linear in the body's size). On the GPU, `rest_offset` grows from `vec2` to `vec4` (rest offset, `e`), 16 B per slot, and `body_spawn` carries `e` with each particle.
8. **The push.** In `contact_delta`, a particle that isn't a body member (a grain or a liquid particle) finds its nearest body particle j within one spacing. With R the body's rotation from shape matching (`frame.zw`), E = R e_j, N = E / |E|, its depth inside the outline is d = |E| − N · (x − x_j). If d > 0, it is pushed by d · N, scaled by `CONTACT_RELAX` like the other contacts and not averaged for liquids. Only that particle moves: the body feels the grain through the ordinary contacts. At rest a grain on a body sits a full spacing from its nearest member, so d < 0 (−0.5 spacing on a flat face, about −0.2 beside a corner, whose outward normal is diagonal) and the push-out never touches a resting contact.
9. **Liquid gets the same push-out**, not only grains: a water particle inside a lattice square is stuck for the same reason, and water staying out of bodies is the step 4 goal.
10. **An erased body loses its push-out.** The eraser changes the outline, and the GPU doesn't know which members went. `brush_erase` marks a body whose member it erases (`info.w` bit 0, "outline stale", set with `atomicOr`), and the push-out skips stale bodies; the ordinary contacts still apply. The CPU reference does the same in `erase_particles`. Cutting (SPEC 2.2) would work the outline out again.
11. **Body–body pairs get the push-out too** (*changed 2026-10-13*, when the owner reported that solids don't collide properly). A body member takes item 8's push-out from its nearest particle of another body, scaled by its share of their contact (`stack_weight`, by density and centroid height as in the ordinary contact). Each side reads only the other body's frame, and each takes its share, so the pair separates by the depth. A member that has passed the nearest member's edge row (it lies on the inner side of it along the outline's normal) also loses that one pair's ordinary push, which would drive it on inward. Before this, the ordinary contacts were all a body pair had, and they balance among two interleaved lattices: a 16 × 10 carrot dropped 150 px onto another (~240 px/s at the impact, over a spacing per substep) ended with 240 of its 320 members inside the other, for good (`body_probe boxdrop`; the same on the CPU, `test_box_dropped_on_box_stays_out`). Without the edge-row rule, at 4 substeps the same drop locked with the rows 0.27 px apart: the pair pushed 1.73 px in against the outline's 1.27 out. The rule drops only that pair, and only past the edge row: dropping every body pair whenever the push-out acted tilted `stack3` to 0.044 rad, from 0.011, because an offset stack's corner members sit just inside the outline under load.
13. **A push between bodies may stop a body but not send it away** (*changed 2026-10-13*, pre-stabilization as in Macklin et al. 2014, "Unified particle physics for real-time applications"). Each body member adds up, over the iterations of a substep, the part of its correction that comes from other bodies (their contacts and item 11's push-out; `solve_delta`, in the slot buffer `vel`, which is free from `hash_scatter` to `velocity_commit`), and after each shape matching every member holds its body's mean (`body_match`). At the velocity update, along that push's direction n, the body may be slowed to 0 by it but keeps none of it as speed away: v·n becomes v·n − |push|/dt if that is still ≥ 0, else min(v·n, 0). From 330 px (main.tscn's Drop buttons, ~390 px/s, 1.6 spacings a substep at 2 substeps), a 16 × 10 carrot dips ~5 px into the one below for a frame; undoing that came back as speed, it bounced up at 78 px/s, tilted and slid off (`body_probe boxhigh`; CPU `test_fast_box_drop_stacks_off_centre`, 73 px/s). Tried first: a per-member cap relative to the other body (no change: only the bottom row touches, and shape matching hands the whole body its push); taking the whole push out of the speed (the box sank 10 px in: the push is what stops it); the push-out alone without the contacts (a 57 px/s rebound was left). Per body, not per member, for the same reason as the cap.

## Considered and rejected
- **Bodies as liquid volume in the grid** (counted in the liquid channel). The grid can't tell a body from water: a dry body on the floor reads as a water column with hydrostatic pressure, and a floating body as a mound of water up to its top, whose extra pressure under it grows as it rises (worked through on paper, not run).
- **The grid's push on every body particle (−∇p dt² / ρ_b)**, with body cells as bed. Bed cells carry a hydrostatic pressure even when dry (it bootstraps from the floor up), so a dry body on the floor or on dry sand would be lifted. Fixing that in the bed cells changes the tuned sand-and-water scenes, and `hydro_solve` precomputes each cell's update from φ alone, so a rule that depends on p can't go into it.
- **Buoyancy from contacts and mass ratios alone** (FleX 7.1). It relies on depth pressure being carried by PBF compression; here the grid carries it, and water at rest is at rest density, so there is nothing to push the body up.
- **A per-body buoyancy force in the body pass** (count submerged members, push all of them up). Needs a "submerged" test per member, which the grid can't give for small bodies, and loses the righting torque.
- **For the push-out: the direction from the grain to the body centroid, or from the members around it.** The centroid points out the wrong side for a grain near a long box's end; the members around a grain inside a lattice square are what cancel today.
- **The push-out shared with the body (mass-weighted).** The grain would need the count of members it sits among to split the push, which it doesn't know; the ordinary contacts already carry the impact to the body.

## Known limits
- ~~A soft body crumples under water.~~ Fixed by item 12. Before it, the 16 × 10 jelly main.tscn drops (density 1.1), sunk in an 80 px pool, ended 4.4–5.0 px off its rest shape with 20–26 water particles inside its outline (most 46 at a sample; `body_probe waterjelly`, three runs); with the push switched off, 2.4 px and 10 inside.
- **A jelly can float in a narrow, shallow pool.** Dropped 4 px onto a 40 px deep pool from wall to wall of a 64 px world (CPU reference), the 16 × 10 jelly (density 1.1) never sank: it rode the ±7 px slosh its plunge set off at the surface, tumbling, for 10 s, with item 12 or without it. Not seen in the 80 px pool of `body_probe` or in main.tscn, where it reaches the floor ~5 s after it is let go. Not looked into; the CPU test lets it go on the floor of the pool instead.
- **A sunk body slides on a film of water.** Nothing squeezes the last layer of water from under a body (compression pushes the film out only where a cell is full), so a carrot on the floor of a pool rests 0.1–2.2 px above it and slides with the pool's currents at 1–7 px/s for seconds after its plunge (`body_probe watersink`; it tumbles as it sinks at ~20 px/s and lands on any face).
- **A box only just stable upright rocks or floats tilted.** A box of density ρ floats upright if width / height > √(6ρ(1 − ρ)) (1.22 at 0.5); the 8 × 6 cork is 1.33 and settles anywhere within ±0.4 rad on the GPU (0.06–0.39 over six runs), on the CPU ±0.25 in a 48 px pool whose slosh keeps moving it. A 16 × 10 cork (1.6) floats within 0.1 rad.
- **A big floater heaves with the pool.** An 80 × 40 px cork set on an 80 px deep wall-to-wall pool plunges and sets off the pool's symmetric slosh (both ends rise and fall together, ~2.8 s); it heaves with it, ±5 px decaying to ±0.5 px over ~12 s, and still moves at 2.6–6 px/s at 13 s. Three times the body drag (60) didn't change it: the drag pulls a body toward the water's velocity, so it can't damp a motion the water has.
- The floor under a wet body is wet (item 4) whether or not water can reach it.
- Bodies drag water nowhere: the drag acts on the body only, so a moving body doesn't pull the water around it along.
- **A body dropped fast onto another dips into it for a frame** (items 11 and 13). Dropped 330 px (main.tscn's Drop buttons, ~390 px/s at the impact), a 16 × 10 carrot moves 1.6 spacings a substep and its bottom rows go up to 5 px into the lower one for about a frame, tilting it ~0.2 rad before it settles level on top (it used to bounce off; item 13). 4 substeps stop the dip at twice the solver's cost; SunaEngine's swept member contacts would stop it at 2 (`docs/papers.md`, Soft & rigid bodies). Faster bodies (up to MAX_STEP, 480 px/s at 2 substeps) dip deeper.

## Decided by the tests
CPU reference (`tests/test_bodies.gd`, 15 tests; the 8 dry ones give their exact numbers from before):
- **Sinking:** an 8 × 6 carrot (density 1.2) dropped into a 48 × 36 px pool rests with its lowest particle 2.15 px above the floor (one water layer), no water inside at any sample, rotation 0.002 rad.
- **Floating:** an 8 × 6 cork (0.5) set on the water: mean draft 0.67 of its height over the last second, 2.6 px/s, tilt up to 0.25 rad, no water inside. Let go on the floor of the pool, it rises and floats at draft 0.55, 0.9 px/s, tilt 0.05 rad. Before `BODY_SURFACE_DROP` the risen cork floated at draft 0.16.
- **Soft body under water (item 12):** a 16 × 10 jelly let go on the floor of a 64 × 40 px pool, water over it, keeps its shape within 0.45 px over the last 2 s of 4 s (1.36 px dry on the floor), no water inside at any sample, 1.9 px above the floor (one water layer), 0.56 px/s. Without item 12: 2.73 px, up to 22 water inside, lifted 4.9 px off the floor at 6.5 px/s.
- **Push-out:** an 8 × 6 carrot dropped 100 px onto a sand bed (~280 px/s at impact) ends with 0 grains inside (at most 1 in a frame; 7 before, from 72 px). The rest outline of a box runs half a spacing outside its boundary members, and erasing part of a body marks it stale.

GPU (`tools/body_probe.gd`, 2026-10-12, RTX 3080 Ti; GPU runs differ from run to run):
- All seven dry scenarios pass with ADR 0002's numbers; `reuse` passes.
- **Sand:** an 8 × 6 box dropped 100 px onto the bed: 0 grains inside (16 before), rotation 0.19 rad, at rest. Placed 2 px above it (`sandlow`, and seven along the bed, `sandrow`): 0 inside, tilts as before (−0.18 to 0.11 rad).
- **Water** (an 80 px pool wall to wall): the carrot sinks to the floor (0.5–2.2 px above it) with no water inside at any sample. Corks: 8 × 6 set on the water (`waterfloat`) draft 0.54–0.57, 0.3–1.6 px/s, tilt 0.23–0.39 rad; dropped from 60 px (`waterdrop`) 0.55–0.59, 0.7–2.2 px/s, tilt 0.06–0.30 rad; 16 × 10 (`waterbox`, main.tscn's size) 0.48 twice, 0.8–2.1 px/s, tilt under 0.1 rad; 80 × 40 (`waterbig`, 1500 frames) 0.61–0.64, 1.3–2.6 px/s. No water inside any of them at any sample.
- **Jelly** (`waterjelly`, item 12): shape error 0.19–0.29 px over five runs (5.0 px at d855c19 the same day), 0 water inside at the end of every run; one water particle inside the outline at one 10-frame sample in two of the five (when wasn't caught: the two runs that printed every such sample had none), so the probe's "none at any sample" check fails now and then on that one particle. Lowest particle 1.90–1.99 px above the floor; 0.7–4.3 px/s at the end, the slide on a water film (Known limits). In main.tscn two dropped jellies sink and rest as clean 16 × 10 boxes; before, both crumpled into ragged blobs with water pockets. The dry scenarios, `sand`, `watersink` and `waterbox` give the same numbers as before; `many` costs the bodies passes 40 µs against 48 µs at d855c19 (noise: the projection's two extra one-workgroup-per-body dispatches per frame don't show).
- **Body on body** (item 11, 2026-10-13): a 16 × 10 carrot dropped 150 px onto another (`boxdrop`) rests on it, 1.86 px apart, no member inside at any sample (before: 240 of 320 inside, for good). Turned 30° and 6 px off centre (`boxtilt`), up to 12 members dip in (2.7 px) at the impact for 2 samples, against 132 (10 px) for 12 samples before; it then tips off the lower box, as a corner landing that far out can. From 330 px, 4 px off centre (`boxhigh`), it bounced off (78 px/s up); with item 13 it stays on top, rebounding at ~4 px/s, and rests 1.87 px apart, after up to 60 members dip in (5.1 px) in 2 samples (the depth allowed there is 3 spacings, Known limits). Before item 11, 140 members ended inside. The dry scenarios, `sand`, `sandrow`, `watersink`, `waterfloat`, `waterjelly` and `many` pass with their numbers from before (`stack3` 0.011 rad, as before).
- `BODY_LIQUID_DRAG` 20 against 60: no difference that matters (the 8 × 6 corks rested tilted 0.37–0.41 rad at both, `waterbig` heaved at 5.9 and 7.4 px/s), so it stays 20.
- **Nothing changes without bodies:** 50k sand at rest (drift 0.000, top grains on grains, no wall grains rising) and sand into water (`water,sand 1800 2`) match e7abc57 within noise; 30k water costs 677 µs per frame against 679 (`--disable-vsync`, from frame 1800); 256 boxes (`many`) cost the bodies pass 42 µs, as before.
