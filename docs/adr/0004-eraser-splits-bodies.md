# 0004: A body the eraser leaves in pieces becomes one body per piece

Date: 2026-10-13. Status: accepted (owner decision), implemented 2026-10-13 (CPU reference and GPU; see "Decided in the implementation"). Milestone 3, before the body brush (step 5).

## Context
The owner erased the middle of a carrot, a jelly and a cork (main.tscn's Drop buttons, right-click eraser): the two parts left kept moving as one body, linked across the gap. Shape matching fits one position and one rotation to every live member of a body id (ADR 0002 items 5 and 8), so pieces that share an id move as one rigid (or one soft) body. ADR 0002 listed this as a known limit until cutting exists, and SPEC §9 deferred "cutting and fracture" past v1.

The body brush (step 5) will let players draw bodies freehand and erase parts of them, so a body left in pieces will be common, and pieces that hang together across empty space look broken.

## Decision
1. **When the eraser leaves a body in two or more pieces, each piece becomes its own body.** A piece is a group of the body's remaining members connected through members that touch on the rest lattice. Each piece then moves, falls, floats and turns on its own, keeping the motion it had at the moment of the erase (no jump, no kick).
2. **This is part of v1 (Milestone 3)**, owner's decision, 2026-10-13: "erasing middle and seeing two parts fall separately should be added now".
3. **A cutting tool and fracture stay deferred** (SPEC §9). Only the eraser splits bodies. A later cutting tool would split bodies the same way, so the split is written as a body operation the eraser calls, not as eraser-only code.

## Consequences
- A split needs a free body id and member range per new piece (ADR 0002 items 4 and 16); the body cap (`body_cap`) and the member list can run out like any other body creation.
- Each piece needs its own rest centroid, its outline (`rest_offset.zw`, ADR 0003 item 7) worked out again, which also clears the stale-outline flag the eraser sets today (ADR 0003 item 10), and its own reference pressure and wet flag (ADR 0003 item 4).
- Contacts, the push-out and the swept contacts between bodies (ADR 0003 items 11, 13, 14) must treat pieces as separate bodies from the substep they split, and must not throw the pieces apart where they still touch along the cut.
- ADR 0002's known limit "A body erased in two stays one body until cutting exists" is retired by this ADR once implemented.

## Decided in the implementation (2026-10-13)
These were open when the owner made the decision; the implementation settled them as below. The ones the owner sees (tiny pieces, a full body cap) are the cheapest defaults and can be changed without touching the rest.
1. **Pieces** are found on the rest lattice, not from current positions: members within 1.5 spacings of each other in their rest offsets are connected (diagonals included), the neighbour rule `rest_outline` already uses for boundary members (`ParticleStep.body_pieces`). A soft body squeezed so two pieces touch still splits where its rest shape is cut.
2. **The split is one body operation** for both sims: `ParticleStep.split_body` in the CPU reference (which `erase_particles` calls on every body it cut, at once), and `ParticleSim.plan_split` plus `body_split.glsl` on the GPU. A cutting tool would call the same operation.
3. **The largest piece keeps the id** (ties: the piece holding the lowest member) and moves to the front of the body's member range. Each other piece takes the lowest free id and a member range by first fit, and copies the body's frame (centroid, rotation), rest centroid, reference pressure and wet flag. Every particle keeps its own position and velocity, so nothing jumps. Rest offsets aren't rewritten: the rest centroid is the live members' (ADR 0002 item 5).
4. **Every piece's outline is worked out again** (`rest_outline` of the piece), which clears `BODY_STALE`: after the split, the push-out (ADR 0003 items 8–11) works on each piece again, also on a body the eraser only nibbled (one piece). Before, an erased body lost its push-out for good.
5. **On the GPU, the split runs on the CPU after readbacks** (no GPU allocation, ADR 0002 item 4). After any erase the body records are read back as before; a body with `BODY_STALE` set and live members gets its member range (`body_members`) and every slot's flags (`mat_flags`, 4 B a slot) read back next; the CPU plans the pieces and sends one entry per live member (slot, old id, new id, member index, outline, piece size) through `body_spawn`; `body_split.glsl` (first in the `brush` pass) moves a member to its new id only while its slot is still alive and still carries the old id, so slots erased or reused since the readback are left alone. A split shares `body_spawn` with a body spawn, so it waits for a frame without one. A split shows 6 frames after the erase on the PC (`body_probe erasemid`).
6. **A stroke dragged across a body** splits it while the stroke goes on: each readback round plans what it sees, and the eraser can only cut pieces further, so the next round splits those. If the eraser ran between the readback and the split, the outlines stay stale until a round with no erase in between (the push-out is off for those frames, as before).
7. **A tiny piece stays a small body**, even one particle (it then moves as a grain that follows the body rules). Not turned into a powder (there is no matching powder material) and not erased (the eraser would take more than the player swept).
8. **When no body id or member range is free, the piece stays on the old body**, linked as before, with its own outline. The erase isn't refused. The next erase on that body tries again.

## Verified (2026-10-13)
- CPU reference (`tests/test_bodies.gd`): a 16 × 10 carrot on the floor with its two middle columns erased becomes two 70-member bodies; lifting the right one (120 px/s up) raises it 13.4 px in 10 frames while the left one moves 0.0001 px and doesn't turn. A falling box cut in two falls on as an uncut control box does, within 0.05 px/s and 0.002 px. Outline of an uncovered member: half a spacing, outward. The GPU planner: ids, ranges, record entries, and a piece that stays attached when no id is free.
- GPU (`body_probe`, RTX 3080 Ti): `erasemid` two 40-member bodies, records' live counts match, no stale outline, shape error 0.0001 px, at rest (fastest piece 1.35 px/s right after the erase). `erasebridge`: a 30 × 5 carrot plank on two pillars, cut in the middle: both halves tip into the gap (rotations 1.57 and 3.14 rad, down 16 and 24 px); before, the halves stayed level on the pillars, linked across the gap (screenshots of main.tscn). `erasedrag`: a 6 px eraser dragged through a box over 20 frames: two 50-member bodies at rest. `erase` (half erased) and `reuse` (erased whole, id reused) unchanged.
- `erasejelly`: the jelly's halves (a lens-shaped cut leaves a 3-column waist) fall over sideways, ~90°. That is the shape, not the split: the same halves made directly as bodies fall over the same way (CPU: −1.571/+1.571 rad, 40.8 px/s whole; −1.554/+1.554 rad, 33.4 px/s split), and the carrot halves stand in both.
