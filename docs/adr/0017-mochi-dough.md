# 0017: Mochi dough, a cohesive lump with no rest shape (Milestone 3b)

Date: 2026-10-17. Status: **proposed, owner to play.** Owner, 2026-10-17: "dough will be mochi-like". The shipped dough row moves from a plastic soft body (ADR 0012) to a new behaviour class, **mochi**, after Phyzios' Mochi (`docs/phyzios.md`, write-up § 5; `task-b18/SPEC.md` § 5.1). Built and measured on the CPU reference and the GPU probe the same day (Results).

## Context
- **What dough was:** a cluster soft body (stiffness 0.8, region 1.5, plasticity 1; ADR 0012) on regions: a rest lattice that plasticity moves past a yield, kept between 0.7 and 1.5 spacings, with the outline following. It keeps dents, but it squeezes instead of spreading, can't tear (the lattice band keeps every link) and can't merge with another dough (two bodies), and a 2000-member dough at low stiffness keeps moving its rest shape (ADR 0012 Known limits, § Dough on pair joints).
- **What the owner asked for:** dough like Phyzios' mochi. A soft, sticky lump with no rest shape: it slumps and flows slowly, stretches into strands when pulled apart, and bonds that break re-form when pieces touch, so torn pieces merge.
- **Phyzios' Mochi** (engine-verified, SPEC § 5.1): `mochiIteration` (3) passes; each pass sets `p_k = Σ w` over all pairs (w ≈ 1 − r/h, a linear hat), then for each pair with a Mochi end and w > 0: `f = (p_i + p_j − 2ρ0)·ke + w·ks` (ke 0.1, ks 0.2), halved when negative, applied to both ends equal and opposite, to position and velocity alike. A pair pulls only while sparser than rest and stops at one kernel radius (4/3 of the rest spacing), so a stretched strand breaks there and re-forms on any new touch. Phyzios is sequential (Gauss-Seidel), has no dt and works in kernel radii; its constants don't port (`docs/phyzios.md`, Rules).

## Decision
1. **A new behaviour class, `mochi`** (`MaterialTable.CLASSES` index 5, after gas and static, so the older numbers stay; `CLASS_MOCHI` in `common.glslinc`; `ParticleStep.MOCHI`). Dough's row becomes `"class": "mochi"` (id 6 kept). Why a class, not a third `soft_model`:
   - mochi has no rest shape, so it needs no body: no rest offsets, outline, push-out, regions, body record, sweep or split. As a body, a torn piece would stay one body with the rest of the dough, and everything done per body (the rigid part of its water pushes, the swept-contact rewind, the push from other bodies spread over the body, STACK_K's body centroid) would tie pieces 100 px apart together; the eraser's split would cut a shape that doesn't exist.
   - With no body, torn pieces are simply apart, and pieces that touch merge with no bookkeeping. A lump isn't bound by the body brush's 2000 cap or the per-body workgroup's cost (ADR 0002): it is painted with the circle brush and filled with "+10k dough", as sand and water are.
   - SPEC 2.2: "New classes are the only thing that needs shader changes", and this one needs its own pair rule. ADR 0016's `soft_model` stays for cluster materials (jelly is on joints, untouched).
2. **The pair rule** (`mochi.glslinc`, `ParticleStep` Mochi section), between two particles of one mochi material, per solver iteration:
   - density `ρ_i = Σ w(r_ij)` over its own-material neighbours, w = 1 − r / R, R = `MOCHI_REACH` 4/3 spacings (Phyzios' kernel at its proportions); ρ0 = 1 on the rest lattice (four neighbours at 0.25);
   - f = (ρ_i + ρ_j − 2 ρ*) · `MOCHI_KE` + w · `MOCHI_KS`, times R, as a move of each end along the pair (> 0 apart); a pull (f < 0) is scaled by `MOCHI_PULL` (0.5, Phyzios' half) × the material's **cohesion**; each end moves at most `MOCHI_MAX_PAIR` (0.25 spacing) per pair;
   - ρ* (`mochi_target`) is ρ0 + w(spacing) · KS / (2 KE), so a pair on the rest lattice pushes by nothing. With ρ0 alone (Phyzios' rule) every pair of a block just made pushes, the lattice grows ~7% and a 20 x 12 block spread from 38 px wide to 58 in half a second (CPU); with ρ*, to 51.
   - Both ends compute f from the same densities and distance, and the move is along the pair, so the moves are equal and opposite: momentum is exact (a Jacobi sum, as ADR 0016's joints), and a resting lump can't creep.
3. **Where it runs on the GPU:** `solve_lambda` stores a mochi particle's density in `lambda` (only liquids use it otherwise, and only liquid neighbours read it); `contact_delta` (`powder.glslinc`) sums the pair rule for own-material neighbours instead of the contact (`mochi_sum`), and `solve_delta` adds it after the half-spacing clamp, as the joints are, which would cut one end's share and not the other's. No new buffer, binding or `Params` field; the material row is unchanged (80 bytes); `common.glslinc` gains `CLASS_MOCHI`, so the shader stamp goes to v26 (SPEC 2.7). `velocity_xsph` adds the liquid's drag on a mochi particle (as on a body, `body_liquid_drag`) and its viscosity (`mochi_viscosity`): a blend toward its own-material neighbours' velocities, weighted by w, of viscosity x `MOCHI_VISC` per 1/60 s over ρ0, the whole blend at most 1.
4. **Against everything else a mochi particle is a grain** (the powder contact rule: mass-weighted push-out, friction, floor and side-wall friction in `solve_apply`, the liquid kick cap in `velocity_update`, no sleeping), with three changes, each found on the GPU probe:
   - **Against a body, heavier by STACK_K when lower**, as a grain is (body_stack_weight with nothing soft), so a dough holds a carrot set on it and squashes under it. Shared by density alone (as a soft body is, ADR 0005 item 11), a carrot dropped 60 px onto a 30 x 12 dough split it and went down to a thin layer on the floor (`doughcarrot`).
   - **Against a grain (or another mochi material), by density alone**, not STACK_K. A mochi pull presses a grain between two dough particles, and with STACK_K their contacts pushed the upper one away more than the lower one: a clump of dough and sand rose through the air at 22 px/s to the ceiling (`doughsand` at friction 0), the lift ADR 0011 found for sticky pulls. Density shares keep momentum, so a pull can't lift anything. The cost: a dough dropped from high up sinks further into a loose pile (Known limits).
   - **The push-out from a body acts only inside the nearest member's lattice cell** (`in_lattice_cell`, as for joint members and grains beside soft bodies). The push-out moves a particle off a carrot with no reaction on the carrot, and the pull draws it back, so a dough holding a carrot crept sideways with it at 0.07-0.18 px/s (`doughcarrot`, `doughwrap`); gated, 0.003 px/s, and no dough inside the carrot.
   In the liquid's density and the density projection grid a mochi particle counts as a body particle does (displacing share 1; body volume and weight channels), so water doesn't fill a dough. It takes the liquid's drag as a body particle does. It gets no grid pressure push, so it sinks through water whatever its density, as grains do (Known limits).
5. **Sliders** (`PANEL_FIELDS["mochi"]`): density, friction, viscosity (how slowly it flows), drag, cohesion (how hard it holds together: the pull; 0 is a lump with no pull, which spreads flat) and adhesion (ADR 0011, unchanged: sticks to other materials and walls). A mochi row's cohesion is its pull, not ADR 0011 stickiness: `stick_k` gives 0 between two particles of one mochi material, and `has_stickiness` and `ParticleSim._note_sticky` don't count it, so the shipped table still has no stickiness and the stick loop costs nothing (dough's adhesion is 0). No mochi-only range: every end was probed (Results). The dough row: density 1.1, friction 0.6, viscosity 0.5, drag 0.6, cohesion 1, adhesion 0, stiffness 0 (unread); `region` and `plasticity` dropped (cluster columns).
6. **Plasticity stays** (ADR 0012): it is code for any soft cluster material with `plasticity` above 0, and the panel's plasticity slider still reads it (jelly at 0 by default; the probes' `set=jelly.plasticity=` and `@plastic` use it). Nothing shipped uses it now; it isn't deleted. FastLSM (ADR 0014) now serves no shipped row either (it is the regions model; jelly is on joints).
7. **Probes** (`tools/body_probe.gd`, scenarios `dough*`, a DOUGH line every 60 frames): `doughslump`, `doughmerge`, `doughcut` (cut with the eraser, then a block dropped across the cut), `doughdrip` (a slab over a gap: sags, strands, tears), `doughcarrot`, `doughsticks`, `doughwrap` (a carrot buried in dough), `doughwater`, `doughsand` and `doughsandlow` (dropped onto a sand pile, and set down on it), `doughcap` (2000) and `doughhuge` (10000). Rest is read on the 99th-percentile speed: a lump that still creeps moves by single particles hopping past their neighbours, 10-25 px/s for a sample.

## What was borrowed from Phyzios, and what wasn't
- **Borrowed:** the pair rule's form (density sum over a linear hat; push where dense, pull where sparse; pull halved; no rest shape; break at the kernel radius, re-form on touch); the kernel's proportions (R = 4/3 spacing, ρ0 = 1 on the square lattice); KE 0.1, KS 0.2 as starting points, which also ended as the values.
- **Not borrowed:** Gauss-Seidel order (a Jacobi sum keeps momentum exactly, and at twice KE and KS the Jacobi sum jittered: vmax ~18 px/s for good, CPU); Phyzios' 3 passes (it runs every solver iteration, 4 per substep x 2); position-and-velocity push (our PBD correction already becomes velocity, ADR 0016 item 4); a density over all neighbours (only its own material counts, so a dough in water or against a carrot still sees its own surface; and only mochi pairs pull, where Phyzios' Mochi pulls on anything it touches: sticking to other things is ADR 0011's adhesion); ρ0 as the target (ρ*, item 2). No code or file from the game.

## How it's judged
GPU probe (RTX 3080 Ti, `--disable-vsync`), the `dough*` scenarios at the shipped row and at both ends of every dough slider, plus the shipped materials' body scenarios against the unchanged build. **Kill criteria:** if a lump creeps or never comes to rest, if pieces don't merge on touch, if any slider end launches or explodes particles, or if a 2000-particle dough costs more a frame than a 2000-member jelly, put dough back on ADR 0012's row (one line) and leave the class unused.

## Known limits
- **A lump holds only ~25-30 px of height** (12-15 rows) and slumps to it in about a second, then creeps slowly: a 30 x 20 block ends ~2x as wide and 0.7x as tall, a 50 x 40 one dropped from mid-air a 300 x 32 px pancake. The pair rule's stiffness is at the Jacobi limit: twice KE and KS jittered (vmax ~18 px/s for good, CPU), and the full pull (`MOCHI_PULL` 1) was barely taller on the GPU (27 against 25 px) and jittered at 6-7 px/s. A coloured solve (`docs/papers.md`, SunaEngine) would allow a stiffer rule, as for joints (ADR 0016 Known limits).
- **A dough dropped from high up onto loose sand makes a crater** (119 of 400 particles more than 3 spacings under the pile's top after a 300 px/s landing); set down, it rests on top. At friction 0 it mixes into the pile.
- **In water a dough sinks whatever its density**, as grains do (no grid pressure push; a body gets its buoyancy from its wet surface, and mochi has no outline for one), at ~80-100 px/s, lands flattened and takes ~10% water into its cracks.
- **It sticks to other things only through adhesion** (ADR 0011), which is weak beside the mochi pull: a 3-row dough at adhesion 1 doesn't hang from the ceiling (CPU), and any adhesion turns the stick loop on (~60-70 µs a frame at 50k) and makes still particles read 5-13 px/s. Phyzios' Mochi pulls on anything it touches; that would need a density for every particle next to a dough.
- **Single particles hop** past their neighbours while a lump creeps (10-25 px/s for a sample), and resting particles shake in place by ~0.05 px a frame (1-4 px/s on the CPU).
- The GPU finds pairs in the hash window, the CPU within H of where particles were at the frame's start: a pair that closes from over H to under the reach within one substep's iterations pulls on the GPU only.
- Not checked: phones, dough with oil, the eraser dragged through a lump in `main.tscn`, a dough in deep water. Flour + water → dough (Milestone 7) and a grab tool don't exist yet.

- **Is it mochi-like?** Play: paint dough blobs (circle brush) and paint one beside another (they merge); "+10k dough"; drop a carrot on a dough mound (body brush); paint dough over a tall carrot and erase through a lump (the pieces stay apart until they touch again). Sliders: viscosity 1 (slower, a little firmer), cohesion 0.3 (runnier) and 0 (falls apart), adhesion 0.5 (clings to carrots and walls, longer strands), friction 1 (firmer).
- **If it is too flat or too runny**, say "firmer": the next lever is a coloured solve for a stiffer pair rule, not a constant.
- **Keep or revert:** one row in `data/materials.json` brings back ADR 0012's dough (`"class": "cluster"`, stiffness 0.8, region 1.5, plasticity 1, viscosity 0, cohesion 0); the class stays for any other row.
- Plasticity (ADR 0012) and FastLSM (ADR 0014) now serve no shipped row; both stay until the owner decides.

## Considered and rejected
- **A third `soft_model`, "mochi", on a cluster body** (the suggested shape): a torn dough would stay one body, so everything done per body (the rigid part of water pushes, the swept-contact rewind, the push from other bodies spread over the body, STACK_K's centroid) would tie its pieces together; the outline, push-out, regions and the eraser's split would all have to be switched off for it, and bodies of dough couldn't merge. A body also caps a lump at the brush's 2000 and costs a workgroup walking it.
- **Phyzios' target ρ0**: every pair of a block just made pushes (the lattice grows ~7%), and a 20 x 12 block spread from 38 px wide to 58 in half a second (CPU; 51 with ρ*).
- **Stiffer constants** (KE 0.2, KS 0.4) and **the full pull**: Jacobi jitter (Known limits).
- **A larger reach** (2 spacings, H): it packed to 1.5 px spacing and kept collapsing at 4 px/s (CPU).
- **Fading the rule over the last tenth of the reach** (against particles flicking at the edge of reach): runnier (148 x 19 px against ~110 x 26), and the hops stayed.
- **Coulomb friction between mochi pairs** (a yield stress): 3-6% narrower mounds on the CPU; not worth a second rule. **Viscosity on the position correction** (XSPH on the move): no better at 0.25, unstable at 1.
- **Density shares against bodies** (as soft bodies have): a dropped carrot split the dough to the floor. **STACK_K against grains**: the mid-air lift (Decision 4).
